# Sweep authored profiles to build BlueCurl's barrel

BlueCurl should build its surfable wave the way every documented production rig has: a **parametric swept-profile mesh**. Interpolate a small library of 2D cross-section keyframes (swell, steepen, throw, plunge, collapse) by break phase. Sweep them along the crest with a phase that shifts linearly with crest position, and evaluate everything statelessly in a WebGL2 vertex shader with a bit-exact JavaScript mirror for the board. *Surf's Up*, *Moana*, Riot's 2XKO shore wave and the academic procedural breakers all converge on this pattern, and it alone meets all of BlueCurl's requirements at once. Those are an overhanging lip, a barrel a camera can enter, an indefinite peel (the wave is a static shape in a frame moving at peel speed), direct control of height, curl and peel, and zero-latency analytic surface queries. The alternatives fail on at least one requirement that matters. Baked FLIP/VAT caches give the best lip detail but little control, heavy memory and GPU-only geometry. Engine water systems are all heightfields and cannot overturn. Real-time physics (shallow water plus particles, SPH/FLIP/MPM, tall-cell hybrids) either cannot form a tube or needs 10–100× the particle budget a browser can run. Around the new wave, ShoreBreak's FFT ocean, sky, post chain, async probe and above all its thickness-based water optics (`shadeWater`, back-face depth, Beer-Lambert plus forward scatter, which is exactly the "translucent jade lip" mechanism) are worth lifting. Its breaker table, schedule, swash solver and whitewater emitters are coupled to a 0.45 m Nice shore dump and should be reimplemented as patterns. The spike's board model, wave-coordinate trajectory planner and roll-free chase camera carry over once their wave backend is swapped. One major caveat applies: nearly every production source (the Imageworks and Disney PDFs, the 2XKO talk, the Ubisoft "Big Waves" article) was readable here only through search extracts, so the architecture rests on consistent [Reported] evidence, not on primary texts.

Claim labels follow the research notes: **[Verified]** means read in the primary source or the local code; **[Reported]** means taken from a search extract, abstract or secondary source; **[Vendor claim]** means a product's own marketing; **[Assessment]** means engineering inference by the research team or this report.

## Film and game precedent converges on 2D profiles swept along the crest

The closest production precedent is Sony Pictures Imageworks' *Surf's Up* (SIGGRAPH 2007). It built its waves with an **animator-facing procedural rig, not a fluid simulation**. In the paper's words, "predefined 2-D wave profiles create a 4-D sampled surface", interpolated per vertex from time-sampled input profiles, with exposed controls for **lip up/down, lip forward/back, trough depth, shoulder size, tube depth and tube length** [Reported] ([Imageworks PDF](https://www.imageworks.com/sites/default/files/2023-10/making-waves-for-surfs-up.pdf)). The rig computed a **"crash curve" by ray intersection along the lip**, where the lip re-enters the face, and used it to drive whitewater. A "wave rider" helper let animators move a surfboard across the wave while it automatically stayed above water [Reported] (same source). A companion sketch layered **Gerstner wave trains with an adjustable "cuspiness"** as ambient displacement [Reported] ([Carlson PDF](https://www.imageworks.com/sites/default/files/2023-10/wave-displacement-effects-for-Surfs-Up.pdf)). Disney's *Moana* used the same idea a decade later. Its **profile-curve wave deformer**, used in more than 40 shots, interpolated cross-section shapes from any number of key curves and **offset them in time along the wave to produce uneven, sectioning breaks**, with whitewater particles (simulated or procedural) layered on top [Reported] ([Disney PDF](https://media.disneyanimation.com/uploads/production/publication_asset/164/asset/Moana_Crashing_Waves.pdf)). A Bournemouth MSc reimplementation built the wave from **NURBS profile curves as blendshape targets, lofted into a surface**, with an automatic break-location and break-speed control [Reported] ([Fan thesis](https://nccastaff.bournemouth.ac.uk/jmacey/MastersProject/MSc09/Fan/msc_thesis_finellafan.pdf)).

The real-time evidence points the same way. Riot's 2XKO "Scuttler's Strand" shore wave is a **pure vertex-shader 3D displacement built from basic math functions, with no simulation, that lets the wave "roll" and form a barrel** [Reported] ([Epic forum summary](https://forums.unrealengine.com/t/talks-and-demos-rolling-shore-waves-in-2xkos-scuttlers-strand/2705287); [talk recording](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=hc9uqdIpYCs)). It is background scenery on a fighting stage, so it almost certainly never had to hold up with a camera inside the tube [Assessment]. Jeschke, Birkholz and Schumann's 2003 procedural breaker evaluates wave shape and foam as **closed-form functions of time and space, needing no previous time steps** [Reported] ([WSCG PDF](http://wscg.zcu.cz/wscg2003/Papers_2003/K11.pdf)). That property is exactly what an indefinitely running wave needs. Mihalef, Metaxas and Sussman's "slice method" builds 3D breakers from a **library of 2D breaking profiles**, although it uses them to seed a Navier–Stokes solve [Verified, abstract] ([Eurographics DL](https://diglib.eg.org/items/5898eea9-9584-4ce7-ae51-e4b3cf908000)). Commercial surf games describe bespoke morphing meshes, not engine water. Barton Lynch Pro Surfing (Unity) has waves that "travel along a plane and morph as they move" [Reported] ([The Xbox Hub interview](https://www.thexboxhub.com/exclusive-interview-how-to-bring-surfing-to-xbox-with-andrew-west-of-barton-lynch-pro-surfing/)). TransWorld Surf used a parametric model with height, steepness and barrel-size parameters [Reported] ([Surfer Today](https://www.surfertoday.com/surfing/the-story-of-the-transworld-surf-video-game)). True Surf promises a wave that changes "from a spilling wave with no tube to a plunging wave where you can get in the barrel", with a camera that works inside it [Vendor claim] ([Meta blog](https://www.meta.com/blog/true-surf-launch/)). The one article written specifically about real-time surfable waves is Adi Bar-Lev's "Big Waves – Creating Swells, Breaks, and Everything In-Between" (*Game Developer*, February 2008), about Ubisoft's *Surf's Up* game. It is a magazine article, not a SIGGRAPH talk, and its body could not be read [Reported] ([GDC Vault magazine PDF](https://media.gdcvault.com/GD_Mag_Archives/GDM_February_2008.pdf)).

### Baked simulation buys lip detail at the cost of control and queries

Offline FLIP in Houdini is the standard way to simulate a crashing wave [Reported] ([SideFX crashing wave tutorial](https://sidefx.com/tutorials/crashing-wave)). The Three.js side is real: **three-vat plays Houdini-style vertex animation textures, including dynamic-topology fluid meshes, with per-instance time offset and speed** [Verified] ([GitHub: three-vat](https://github.com/manthrax/three-vat)). However, it states no licence and calls itself "still evolving". Fluid-mode VATs have reported problems: high polycounts, per-frame UV unwraps, "broken shading and jittering triangles" and no LOD story [Reported] ([SideFX forum](https://www.sidefx.com/forum/topic/78393/); [VAT 3.0 docs](https://www.sidefx.com/docs/houdini/nodes/out/labs--vertex_animation_textures-3.0.html)). Simulations do not loop natively, and the standard workaround is a crossfade between time-offset copies [Reported] ([SideFX forum](https://www.sidefx.com/forum/post/463321/)). The research team's sizing puts a fluid-mode VAT barrel at **about 108–216 MB of GPU memory per 2 s loop**. Re-projecting the sim onto a fixed-topology sheet (about 49k vertices) brings that down to **about 12–24 MB** [Assessment]. The deeper problems are control and queries. A bake offers only playback rate, uniform Froude scaling (scale s with time ×√s) and blends between a few variants. Independent peel speed needs a new bake, and board queries need a separately baked CPU proxy because the geometry lives on the GPU [Assessment]. No shipped game was found that rides a baked FLIP barrel; industry practice keeps baked fluid for short one-shot FX and makes traversable water procedural [Assessment, from cases including [Sea of Thieves](https://history.siggraph.org/wp-content/uploads/2022/09/2018-Talks-Ang_The-Technical-Art-of-Sea-of-Thieves.pdf) and [Andreas Glad's VAT splash](https://vimeo.com/266717949)].

### Engine water is heightfield water, and none of it curls

No mainstream engine water system produces an overturning lip. Unity's HDRP documentation lists **"breaking waves on shorelines" as unsupported**. Its Shore Wave deformer is vertical only [Reported] ([HDRP 17 capabilities](https://docs.unity3d.com/Packages/com.unity.render-pipelines.high-definition@17.0/manual/water-capabilities-of-the-water-system.html)). Crest attenuates waves in shallow water and calls realistic shoreline waves "a challenging open problem" [Verified via docs extract] ([Crest docs](https://crest.readthedocs.io/en/stable/user/shallows-and-shorelines.html)). UE5's Water plugin is Gerstner-based [Verified via docs extract] ([Epic docs](https://dev.epicgames.com/documentation/unreal-engine/water-body-actors-in-unreal-engine)). Only paid UE5 plugins claim barrels: Oceanology NextGen advertises "formula-driven breaking waves that rise, barrel, and collapse" on RTX 3080-class hardware [Vendor claim] ([Fab listing](https://www.fab.com/listings/87c9af41-62b7-4e70-98e3-fc72eff016ab)). Neither engine is a browser path. HDRP does not target the web, and Unreal requires Pixel Streaming at roughly **$0.05–0.25 per user-hour** [Reported] ([Ravespace analysis](https://ravespace.io/blog/pixel-streaming-vs-webgl-three-js-2025-s-scientific-verdict-on-3d-web-technologies)). No public three.js demo of an overturning wave was found [Reported] ([three.js forum](https://discourse.threejs.org/t/make-high-performance-games-with-water-surface-simulations-with-water-bodies/74113)). BlueCurl would be building something without a public web precedent.

### Real-time physics either cannot form a tube or cannot fit the budget

The most direct real-time precedent for physically driven overturning is Thürey et al. 2007. The method detects steep fronts in a shallow-water heightfield, spawns **sheets of connected particles as the lip**, and converts them to spray and foam on impact [Reported, abstract] ([ResearchGate](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/220936975_Real-time_Breaking_Waves_for_Shallow_Water_Simulations)). It yields a thin curtain, not a tube interior, and its height and curl are controlled only indirectly through bathymetry. It is also covered by **NVIDIA's US patent 8,204,725**, which the research team estimates runs to about 2028; that estimate is unverified [Verified, patent exists] ([Google Patents](https://patents.google.com/patent/US8204725B1/en)). Wave particles, water surface wavelets and boundary-aware procedural waves are excellent heightfield models, but they cannot overturn [Verified] ([Wave Particles](https://cemyuksel.com/research/waveparticles/); [Water Surface Wavelets](https://research-explorer.ista.ac.at/record/134); [boundary-aware waves](https://diglib.eg.org/handle/10.1111/cgf14100)). Browser particle fluids top out at **about 100k particles on integrated GPUs and 300k on mid-range GPUs** with WebGPU MLS-MPM [Vendor claim] ([Codrops](https://tympanus.net/codrops/2025/02/26/webgpu-fluid-simulations-high-performance-real-time-rendering/)). The lip of a 3 m barrel over 20 m of crest alone needs **about 144k particles at 5 cm spacing and 1.15M at 2.5 cm**, and 5 cm particles look blobby at the under-2 m range a barrel camera reaches [Assessment]. The NVIDIA tall-cell and Eulerian–heightfield–particle hybrids handle local 3D breaking on desktop GPUs, but they have no browser port and make waves *emerge* from forcing rather than *obey* parameters [Verified, abstracts] ([SCA 2014](https://diglib.eg.org/handle/10.2312/sca.20141117.001-010)). No 2015–2026 paper surfaced showing a real-time simulated surfable barrel [Reported].

| Approach | Overhang and enterable barrel | Indefinite steady peel | Control of height, curl, peel | Browser cost | Analytic board query |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Swept-profile parametric (Surf's Up, Moana, 2XKO, Jeschke 2003) | Yes; the tube is authored geometry | Yes; static in the peel frame | Direct parameters | Low (vertex shader, about 10⁵ vertices) [Assessment] | Exact, via a JS mirror of the profile |
| Baked FLIP to VAT or mesh cache | Yes, the richest detail | Only with a moving-frame crossfade loop | Playback rate, scale, a few blends | 12–216 MB per 2 s loop [Assessment] | Needs a separately baked CPU proxy |
| Engine water and plugins | No (heightfields); UE plugins only claim it | n/a | Good for swell | Not deliverable to the web without streaming | Gerstner sums only |
| Shallow water plus lip particles (Thürey 2007) | Thin curtain, no tube interior | Hard (inflow and bathymetry) | Indirect | Moderate | Heightfield body only |
| SPH, FLIP or MPM in WebGPU | Emergent | Unreliable (drift, recycling) | Poor | 100–300k particle ceiling, short of surf scale | Needs surface reconstruction and readback |

## The heightfield spike proved the peel, not the foundation

The surf spike showed that **a continuously peeling wave is simply a breaker whose stage depends only on t − x/v_peel**. Adding an unbounded linear peel term `ti(x) = t0 + (dir/v_peel)·x` to ShoreBreak's per-column clock turned each breaker event into a pattern that travels along the coast and is steady in its own frame. That pattern peels indefinitely with no rescheduling [Verified] (`docs/SURF-SPIKE.md` §2). That insight transfers directly to the new wave. What does not transfer is the substrate. ShoreBreak's ocean is a **single-valued heightfield `y = f(x, z)`**, and everything that overturns lives in a separate lip ribbon. That ribbon holds only two slots, spans ±40 m around the focus and takes its cross-section from an **18-knot stage table fitted to one ~0.45 m wave dumping on a Nice pebble beach**, Froude-scaled by `s = H/0.45` [Verified] (`docs/SURF-SPIKE.md` §1; `src/water/LipRibbon.js`, `src/glsl/breaker.js` on `shorebreak-original`). Reaching a bigger wave further out meant moving hard-coded Nice z-gates offshore (crest-optics fades at −13…−23 m, the wave-zone gate at −18 m, the breaker/swell hand-over at −22…−32 m), re-banding the mesh and making the swell field follow the focus. The change touched **16 source files, +1172/−42 lines** [Verified] (`docs/SURF-SPIKE.md` §2; spike diff). In this design the barrel is a seam between two meshes rather than one continuous surface. Its shape comes from extrapolating a shore dump, not from a surf-zone plunger, and the board probe reads the rendered heightfield back **synchronously every frame**, which the spike's own comment calls "a pipeline stall on a real GPU" [Verified] (`src/surf/SurfProbe.js`). Each of these limits stems from the heightfield-plus-ribbon architecture, which is why patching it further is the wrong bet [Assessment].

The spike's surf rig, by contrast, is largely architecture-neutral. The board (`src/surf/Board.js`) is a lofted 6'0" thruster (1.83 m × 0.483 m × 62 mm) lit with the scene's constants and drawn in the opaque pass so the water refracts its wetted bottom [Verified]. The trajectory planner (`src/surf/SurfDemo.js`) plans in **wave coordinates as pure functions of time**: arrival clock `alpha(t)` and face position `h(t)`, cycling cruise → climb → descend → bottom turn → return. It recovers world x by Newton iteration on the arrival clock and z from a CPU mirror of the profile [Verified]. The chase camera sits 4 m behind and 2 m above on exponentially smoothed heading and height, stays at least 0.8 m above the water and **always keeps roll at zero**, which is what keeps the horizon level [Verified] (`docs/SURF-SPIKE.md` §3). The coupling point is narrow: `src/surf/waveGeom.js` imports `brkTiming` and `brkStageJS` from ShoreBreak's schedule and breaker [Verified]. Replacing that one module with the new profile evaluator moves the whole rig across.

## A layered wave: swept body, ballistic lip, persistent foam, thickness-lit jade

The recommended wave is one parametric sheet in coordinates (s along the crest, u along profile arc length). About 6–12 profile keyframes are **resampled to equal arc length with the same vertex count**, so interpolating between keys is a per-vertex lerp or Catmull-Rom. The lip and tube are then just part of the curve, with no special cases. The keys are stored in a small float texture (phase × arc length → x, y) and swept in the vertex shader with `phase(s, t) = f((s − v_peel·t)/L_section + phase0)` plus low-frequency section noise, as *Moana*'s time offsets do [Assessment, following [Disney PDF](https://media.disneyanimation.com/uploads/production/publication_asset/164/asset/Moana_Crashing_Waves.pdf)]. In the frame moving at v_peel the mesh is time-invariant, so "indefinite" costs nothing. Two practical details follow. Run the wave in a rebased local frame, because world x passes 20 km after an hour at 6 m/s and float32 phase and noise lose precision. Render the sheet double-sided, concentrating u-tessellation near the lip so the tube interior holds up at 3 m [Assessment]. The profile keys can start from the *Surf's Up* course-note curves the Fan thesis reused [Reported] ([Fan project](https://nccastaff.bournemouth.ac.uk/jmacey/MastersProject/MSc09/Fan/)), from ShoreBreak's stage table as a seed, or from a cheap offline **2D** plunging-breaker sim. A 2D sim is the most physically grounded option, and it is far cheaper than a 3D bake [Assessment]. Gerstner/trochoid surfaces belong in the swell and ambient layer only, because at high steepness they self-intersect instead of curling [Reported] ([Hinsinger et al. survey](https://www.cg.tuwien.ac.at/courses/Rendering/RealtimeWaveAnimation.pdf)).

**The jade lip is an optics problem ShoreBreak has already solved.** Shipped games fake crest translucency cheaply. Sea of Thieves blends deep and subsurface colours by "view angle, sun direction and a wave peak mask… generated from the FFT chop" [Reported] ([Ang et al. 2018](https://history.siggraph.org/wp-content/uploads/2022/09/2018-Talks-Ang_The-Technical-Art-of-Sea-of-Thieves.pdf)), and Crest exposes an SSS tint and a sun-directed intensity [Vendor claim] ([Crest docs](https://crest.readthedocs.io/en/4.23.0/user/water-appearance.html)). ShoreBreak goes further. A **back-face depth pass measures the real thickness of thin sheets**. It then applies Beer-Lambert transmission of the actual background plus a forward-scatter lobe `0.18 + 0.82·pow(max(dot(−V,L),0),5)`, and lets an analytic `thickHint` override the measured thickness [Verified] (`src/glsl/water.js` ~770–857 on `shorebreak-original`). A swept profile knows its lip thickness exactly, so the new mesh can feed that hint directly. The jade colour then comes from absorption coefficients and in-scatter colour, which need recalibrating from the Nice values for a deeper, bigger wave [Assessment].

**Foam needs a coverage envelope fixed to the peel and texture that moves with the water.** The standard foam technique is a persistence texture: inject foam at crests or where the Jacobian goes negative, then decay it with a half-life [Reported] ([Tessendorf et al.](https://jtessen.people.clemson.edu/reports/papers_files/Os2022_tessendorf_reinhardt_gao.pdf)). Measured whitecap decay times run **0.2–10.4 s**, a usable calibration range [Reported] ([Callaghan et al. 2012](https://data-ww3.ifremer.fr/BIB/Callaghan_etal_JGR2012.pdf)). For a peeling wave, the coverage envelope after the break is steady in the peel frame. The foam texture itself must not slide along the crest, though, because real whitewater moves shoreward with the bore. The right split is an envelope in wave coordinates with detail advected in world or flow coordinates. ShoreBreak's two cross-faded advected material-coordinate sets and its Worley foam bake (`src/glsl/foam.js`, `src/whitewater/foamNoise.js`) supply the look [Assessment, code Verified].

**Spray and the impact field should be emitted from the analytic crash curve.** Tessendorf emits spray where the displacement folds over [Reported] ([course slides](https://jtessen.people.clemson.edu/reports/papers_files/course_slides2002.pdf)). *Surf's Up* drove whitewater from its crash curve [Reported] ([Imageworks PDF](https://www.imageworks.com/sites/default/files/2023-10/making-waves-for-surfs-up.pdf)). For BlueCurl, the profile gives the lip-tip landing line and impact speed in closed form. Ballistic lip-drop and spray particles launched from it fit the browser easily [Assessment]: ShoreBreak's existing GPGPU pool already holds **262,144 particles** (blobs, droplets, ligaments), rendered through order-independent thickness buffers and a crisp droplet pass [Verified] (`src/whitewater/Whitewater.js`). Backlighting spray needs the same forward-scatter phase function as the lip. No shipped-game source on backlit spray shading was found [Gap]. The thin, continuous lip itself must be mesh geometry, not particles, because particle spacing becomes visible from inside the barrel [Assessment].

**Surface queries should be analytic, not read back.** Engines query water three ways. Some evaluate an analytic wave sum on the CPU, as UE's Buoyancy plugin does with a mirrored Gerstner copy [Verified via docs extract] ([FBuoyancyGerstnerWaveData](https://dev.epicgames.com/documentation/unreal-engine/API/Plugins/Buoyancy/FBuoyancyGerstnerWaveData)). Some use asynchronous GPU queries that "can take a couple of frames", as Crest does [Vendor claim] ([Crest docs](https://crest.readthedocs.io/en/4.23.0/user/collision-shape-and-buoyancy-physics.html)). Others sample a smoothed low-rate model, as Atlas does at under 10 Hz [Reported] ([Atlas slides](https://gpuopen.com/gdc-presentations/2019/gdc-2019-agtd6-interactive-water-simulation-in-atlas.pdf)). A swept profile allows the best of these: a **JS evaluator that mirrors the shader bit-exactly**, which is ShoreBreak's own `brkTiming` pattern. Given the board's crest coordinate, the evaluator finds the closest point on the 2D cross-section polyline at the local phase and returns height, normal and **surface velocity** from the time derivative. ShoreBreak's probes never provide velocity, yet the board needs it [Assessment]. Centimetre-scale FFT chop can be ignored for physics or seated visually with ShoreBreak's async `SurfaceProbe`. That probe uses three.js r186's `readRenderTargetPixelsAsync` (PBO plus fence) and extrapolates to hide latency [Verified] (`src/water/SurfaceProbe.js`; `node_modules/three/src/renderers/WebGLRenderer.js`).

**Stay on WebGL2 and treat WebGPU as an upgrade.** ShoreBreak already runs a WebGL2 three-cascade 256² JONSWAP FFT in fragment passes [Verified] (`src/water/OceanFFT.js`). For scale, a Unity FFT ocean reports 1.65 ms for 3×256² cascades on an RTX 4070 [Reported] ([80.lv](https://80.lv/articles/real-time-physically-accurate-ocean-surface-simulation-in-unity)). WebGPU is not automatically faster in three.js: one report measured 2–4× more FPS on WebGL [Reported] ([three.js forum](https://discourse.threejs.org/t/webgpu-performance-issue/87939)). A three.js WebGPU iFFT ocean also failed silently on Safari [Reported] ([three.js forum](https://discourse.threejs.org/t/good-progress-in-developing-a-real-ocean/55834)). WebGPU earns its place later, for compute-driven particles or an optional cosmetic MLS-MPM patch of 100k particles or fewer in the impact zone, seeded from the analytic lip [Assessment].

## Lift ShoreBreak's optics, ocean and sky; rewrite its breaker and emitters

ShoreBreak is MIT-licensed (© 2026 Christopher Canavan) on three 0.186.0 [Verified] (`LICENSE`, `package.json` on `shorebreak-original`). Every reuse below works within its **16-sampler shader budget** (`src/core/shaderBudget.js`). The optics already use opaque colour, opaque depth, back depth, the sky LUT and two ocean arrays, so adding profile and foam textures means dropping the SWE samplers on the new mesh [Assessment]. One rule from ShoreBreak must survive: it hides its lip/heightfield seam by evaluating the *same* `waterParts` displacement on both meshes. The new wave and the surrounding sea need one shared surface-displacement function used by every water mesh and by the probe [Assessment].

These effort ratings come from the research team's reading of headers, imports and uniforms, not of every line [Assessment]. ShoreBreak has **no frame-time measurements** for any subsystem: its README disclaims a fixed FPS, and its release validation took no hardware FPS measurements [Verified] (`README.md`, `docs/RELEASE-VALIDATION.md`). The FFT, the 262k-particle OIT path and the SWE solver therefore need GPU-timer profiling before BlueCurl commits to their per-frame cost.

## Five milestones take the wave from profile texture to barrel ride

The plan orders work so the hardest unknowns (overhang geometry, the barrel camera and the lip look) are proved before the most labour-intensive part (whitewater) is rebuilt. Each milestone ends in a capture that can be judged against the spike's clips.

## Conclusion

The research reframes BlueCurl's problem. The difficulty is not simulating a breaking wave but **authoring one cross-section well and making it fixed in a moving frame**. Once the peel is understood as a coordinate change, which the spike demonstrated, "indefinitely peeling" stops being expensive, and the open questions shrink to profile quality, tube-interior rendering and whitewater density. That moves the project's risk from physics to art direction and optics. ShoreBreak's most valuable asset turns out to be its thickness-based light transport rather than its breaker; it was built for a 0.45 m dump but transfers directly to a jade surf lip.

The practical implication is that a working barrel ride needs no new simulation technology, only discipline about a single source of truth. One profile function, sampled identically by the vertex shader, the JS board query, the camera keep-out test and the crash-curve emitters, keeps geometry, physics and FX consistent by construction. Losing that property is the failure mode that sank the heightfield-plus-ribbon design. Simulation still has a place, but upstream: an offline 2D breaker sim is the cheapest way to make the profile library physically honest, and a baked detail sheet is the fallback if the swept surface reads too clean.
