# 01 — Visual references (Investigation 1)

Project: BlueCurl (real-time rear-chase surfing visualisation, Three.js/WebGL2).
Researched 2026-11-19. Owner: art-direction research. Status: first pass, ready for the mood board.

## How to read this file

- **Labels:** **[Verified]** = I checked the primary source myself (an image URL, file page or licence, a page I fetched).
  **[Verified-URL]** = the direct image URL returned `HTTP 200` + `content-type: image/jpeg` to
  `curl -sI -A "Mozilla/5.0 research-bot"` on 2026-11-19. **[Vendor claim]**, **[Reported]** (reviews, press,
  search-result snippets), **[Assessment]** (my judgement). **[Not viewed]** = link found via search but the host was
  unreachable from the research sandbox, so I did not watch or see it.
- **Direction tags:** **A** "Tropical Clarity", **B** "Golden Hour Cinematic", **C** "Atlantic Drama".
  A proposed fourth direction, **D** "Soft Overcast", is described in section 9.
- **Category tags:** c1 pro surfing, c2 behind/over the surfer, c3 games, c4 human body / rear view,
  c5 boards, c6 apparel, c7 coast, c8 lighting, c9 water/spray.
- **Image IDs** (IMG-01 … IMG-78) point to the embeddable library in section 8. Every IMG entry is a Wikimedia
  Commons file with a CC0, PD, CC BY or CC BY-SA licence. CC BY and CC BY-SA need on-page credit (author + licence + link),
  and the mood-board page should show that credit. CC BY-SA does not force our *code* to be SA, but a derivative
  *image* (crop or grade) would have to be shared under CC BY-SA.

## 2. Behind-the-surfer tracking footage (c2) — the most important category

### 2.1 Rig taxonomy (what each real rig looks like) **[Assessment]**

Values are typical figures from rig geometry and common lenses. They were not measured from the clips.

| Rig | Cam height above water | Distance to surfer | Lens / FOV feel | Horizon | How it handles turns | Usefulness for us |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Water follow-cam** (filmer surfs behind; handheld, pole or mouth-mount GoPro) | 0.4–1.5 m | 1.5–5 m | Ultra-wide 14–16 mm equivalent, ~110–120° H-FOV, barrel distortion | Rolls with the filmer's lean, bounces | The filmer rides a similar line, so the camera traces the surfer's path with a delay. On bottom turns the wave face fills half the frame. Spray and droplets hit the lens. | **Highest.** This *is* a rear chase. Copy the trajectory logic, not the fisheye or the bounce. |
| **Drone follow / chase** (DJI-class, gimbal) | 8–40 m | 15–50 m | ~24 mm equivalent, ~80° H-FOV, rectilinear | Gimbal-locked, perfectly level | The pilot either holds heading (the surfer carves across frame) or arcs with the rider. It lags smoothly and never snaps. | High for the "drop out" and wide establishing moments. Too high for continuous riding. |
| **FPV drone** | 2–8 m | 3–15 m | ~150° fisheye | Banks with the drone | Aggressive banking through turns, very kinetic | Medium. Exciting but reads gamey. Use at most as a short highlight cam. |
| **Jet-ski follow** (tow partner / water safety / filmer on ski) | 1–1.5 m | 10–30 m behind or shoulder | Handheld wide to tele, plus chop bounce | Unstable | The ski stays on the shoulder or behind the foam. Heavy pitch from chop. | Medium. Good reference for *scale* and the camera bobbing in chop between waves (Paddling and Drop-out phases). |
| **Boat in the channel** (e.g. IMG-09 Mavericks) | 2–4 m | 50–150 m | 300–600 mm | Level | Pans and keeps the rider centred | Low for riding, good for wave scale |
| **Beach / tower long lens** (WSL broadcast) | 5–20 m | 100–500 m | 400–1200 mm, compressed | Level | Front or side view, never from behind | Reference for the grade only, not for the camera |
| **Wave-pool auto-tracking cams** (Surfline at Surf Ranch and Waco; Wavegarden "Surf Eye"; URBNSURF) | Fixed poles | Side-on | Tele | Level | Software-tracked pans | Low. Mostly side-on [Reported] |

### 2.2 Recommended starting spec for the BlueCurl chase cam **[Assessment]**

- **Height:** 1.6–2.5 m above the local water surface. This keeps the lens about 0.5–1 m above the surfer's head, so the head never overlaps the horizon. Reduce it to about 0.8–1.2 m in the Paddling phase, as in IMG-03 and IMG-05.
- **Distance:** 4–7 m behind. Let it stretch to about 8–10 m at speed and on airs. Tighten it to about 3 m in the barrel.
- **FOV:** vertical 40–50° (about 28–35 mm full-frame equivalent on 16:9). That is wider than broadcast and narrower than GoPro. **Avoid fisheye barrel distortion.** It is the single biggest "action-cam/gamey" tell.
- **Aim:** look about 1–2 m ahead of the surfer at chest height. Put the surfer's hips about one third up from the bottom of frame and keep the open wave face visible on the wave side.
- **Horizon:** keep it level, like a gimbal. Pass through only 15–30 % of board roll, with heavy damping. Real follow-cam roll looks amateur. Drone footage reads as "cinema".
- **Lag:** use a critically damped spring of about 0.3–0.5 s on yaw and 0.2 s on height. In a bottom turn the trailing camera naturally swings to the outside of the arc and reveals the wave face. This is the best moment real follow-cams get, and it comes free from correct lag.
- **Shutter look:** keep droplets crisp, like a 1/1000 s sports shutter, and apply only subtle camera-motion blur. Surf media freezes spray. Smeared spray reads as CG.

**Finding [Reported]:** I found no published *rear-tracking rail camera* at Surf Ranch. Its automated capture (Surfline) and Wavegarden's AI "Surf Eye" system are fixed tracking cameras that mostly look side-on ([wavepoolmag](https://wavepoolmag.com/surflines-first-b2b-product-targets-wave-pools/), [Wavegarden/Surf Eye](https://wavepoolmag.com/wavegarden-surf-eye-ai-video-system/)). A US patent, "Traveling camera apparatus for surfing" (US20080317451A1, <https://www.google.ca/patents/US20080317451>), shows the idea exists, but I found no broadcast use. **Implication:** continuous smooth rear-chase surfing imagery barely exists in real media. Real footage gives us bursts: follow-cam clips of 5–15 s, and drone passes. Our camera has to combine follow-cam *proximity* with drone *stability*. No single real shot type is a direct template. **[Assessment]**

| Rear-view body refs (embeddable) | IMG-03, IMG-05, IMG-35, IMG-37, IMG-44, IMG-45, IMG-46, IMG-64, IMG-67, IMG-72 | Rear and rear-three-quarter reads: shoulder-blade and lat shape in paddling (IMG-05), wetsuit panel lines from behind (IMG-46), silhouettes against glare (IMG-64, IMG-67). |

**[Assessment] What makes a surfer read as real from behind** (camera 4–7 m back, so the face is almost never visible):
1. **Silhouette and proportions.** Surfers have broad lats and shoulders and a narrow waist, and stand in a wide, low, bent-knee stance. Arms are out for balance.
2. **Secondary motion.** Wet hair clumps, and boardshorts flutter or cling. A wetsuit barely moves; it creases at the knees and lower back.
3. **Specular breakup.** Wet skin and neoprene show broken, streaky highlights from water films, not uniform gloss. Compare IMG-44 (dry-ish matte neoprene with wet patches) with IMG-07 (soaked suit, near-mirror rim highlight).
4. **Contact with the board.** Feet sit under the board's water line and the board is partly occluded by spray. Spray hides foot-contact errors, which works in our favour.

Faces matter only in rare frames. The budget should go to **body, suit/shorts, hair and animation** **[Assessment]**.

## 5. Surfboards (c5)

Embeddable: IMG-38 (waxing, deck, PD), IMG-39 (wood-inlay 9'0" longboard), IMG-40 (rental rack: deck colours, sprays, traction pads), IMG-41 (longboards with resin tints and pinlines), IMG-42 (vintage single-fins, yellowed), IMG-43 (fin in fog), plus board details in IMG-06, IMG-17 and IMG-46.

Design notes for the hero board **[Assessment]** (brand pages could not be checked):
- **A pro shortboard reads as almost white from behind.** It has a clear-glassed PU blank (slightly warm white, which yellows with age as in IMG-42), a small logo, often a spray-painted rail band or bottom colour, a black or dark traction pad on the tail, and visible wax bumps on the deck. A tri-fin (thruster) or quad sits under the tail, which is rarely seen from behind except on airs and snaps.
- **Readability trick:** a coloured *bottom* spray or rail tint only shows in turns and airs. It gives "flashes" of colour exactly at the high-action moments. That is cheap and effective.
- **Shapes to model:** shortboard (5'10"–6'2", thruster), fish (twin keel, swallow tail, for the "relaxed" rider), longboard (9'+, single fin, resin tint, pinlines like IMG-41), and gun (7'+ pintail, for Atlantic Drama / Nazaré). Do not model the Jaws/Nazaré tow boards with foot straps unless tow-in is in scope.
- **Avoid:** saturated toy colours (IMG-40's rental soft-tops), and logo-heavy decks.

## 6. Surf apparel (c6)

Embeddable: IMG-44 (full black wetsuit on an athlete), IMG-45 (women's full suit), IMG-46 (rear three-quarter wetsuit panels and sheen), IMG-47 (O'Neill shorty layout), IMG-05 (bikini, paddling, rear), IMG-35 (bikini, turning, rear three-quarter), IMG-12, IMG-17, IMG-27 and IMG-33 (boardshorts), IMG-14 (rash vest).

**[Assessment] How wet fabric reads:**
- **Neoprene (3/2, 4/3, chest-zip):** dry it is matte charcoal. Wet, the smooth-skin panels (chest/back on winter suits) go near-mirror. Jersey-lined panels stay satin. The panel seams show as thin darker lines (IMG-46). From behind, the key read is the **back panel and the chest-zip flap across the shoulders**, plus creasing at the lower back and the backs of the knees. In direction C the suit should carry the whole specular "story" of the figure.
- **Boardshorts:** wet, they darken by about 30–40 %, cling to the thighs and lose their flutter. Mid-thigh to knee length on pros. Muted solids or simple prints work better than loud patterns at distance.
- **Bikini / one-piece surf suits:** high-contrast colour reads at distance. Wet skin carries the specular.
- **Rash vest / short-sleeve top (IMG-14, IMG-27):** clings and darkens. A good tropical alternative to bare skin, and it needs no skin-shader quality on the back.

## 9. Mapping to the art directions

| Direction | Best refs (in order) | What the refs say that the brief might not expect |
|---|---|---|
| **A Tropical Clarity** | IMG-17, IMG-23, IMG-10, IMG-15, IMG-21, IMG-02, IMG-13, IMG-58 | Measured colours are **teal/jade, not candy turquoise**. Turquoise shows up only where sunlight passes *through* the lip (IMG-17, IMG-15). Deep water reads dark teal to cobalt. Whitewater covers 25–45 % of the frame and is pure white with blown highlights. Shadows are hard, with very blue skies. **Risk:** hard sun exposes every shading and animation flaw on the figure, and our ocean is from another team. |
| **B Golden Hour Cinematic** | IMG-07, IMG-75, IMG-64, IMG-63, IMG-72, IMG-60, IMG-71, IMG-03 | The best B frames are **back-lit**: the camera faces the sun, so the rider is a rim-lit silhouette and spray becomes the brightest element. For a rear-chase camera that means riding *toward* the sun, which suits a fixed sun azimuth if the wave peels toward it. Water goes olive or steel-blue and the sky carries the warmth. **Risk:** bloom and auto-exposure have to be very good. ShoreBreak already has bloom, glare and an exposure meter, which helps. |
| **C Atlantic Drama** | IMG-54, IMG-09, IMG-26, IMG-53, IMG-51, IMG-68, IMG-61, IMG-73, IMG-44 | Slate and steel-blue palette. Scale comes from **foam fields and mist**, not wave height alone. Full black wetsuits help us: neoprene hides skin-shader limits, and the strong silhouette reads well from behind. **Risk:** grey can look "unfinished" in CG unless haze, spray and light shafts (broken cloud) carry it. |
| **D Soft Overcast (proposed alternative)** | IMG-24 (Mentawai under high cloud), IMG-34 (overcast Oahu barrel), IMG-65 (fog surf), IMG-52, IMG-57 | **[Assessment]** This is the cheapest look to make believable in real time. There are no hard contact shadows to get wrong, skin and neoprene speculars are soft, and the sky dome does the lighting. It reads like a real camera and not like a game, because games rarely ship overcast. IMG-24 proves overcast *tropical* water is still beautiful: soft teal, no sparkle. It works as a fallback mood or a weather variant for A/C, and as the "default" while the ocean team integrates. |

**Recommendation [Assessment]:** develop **B** as the hero look, because it plays to the rear chase (rim-lit silhouette, backlit spray), and **C** as the second. Keep **D** as the engineering baseline. Treat **A** as the hardest of the three, not the easiest, despite how commercial it looks.

## 12. Observations for art direction

Recurring traits in the strongest footage and stills **[Assessment]**, with the supporting refs:

1. **The horizon sits high and level.** In rear and water-level shots (IMG-06, IMG-03, IMG-05) the horizon falls at about 55–70 % of frame height, a little above the surfer's shoulders, and it is level. The tilt comes from the *wave face*, not from the camera rolling. → Keep the camera roll-stabilised and get drama from the wave slope.
2. **The surfer is small.** In professional frames the rider fills only 10–25 % of the frame height (IMG-16, IMG-20, IMG-23, IMG-32). The wave and spray are the subject. → Pull back further than games do. This also hides animation and shading flaws.
3. **Backlit spray is the hero highlight.** In B and in many A frames, the brightest pixels are spray and lip, not sky (IMG-07, IMG-17, IMG-75, IMG-64). The spray is crisp, with no motion smear, and has a bright soft halo (bloom) where the sun is behind it.
4. **The silhouette reads first.** From behind, the read is stance plus arms plus board angle. Against glare the figure is nearly black with a thin rim (IMG-67, IMG-64, IMG-03). A strong pose matters more than a detailed texture.
5. **Wet materials show broken speculars.** Neoprene shows streaky highlights along panels and creases (IMG-46, IMG-07). Wet skin shows small, high-intensity glints, not an overall gloss (IMG-05, IMG-35). Wet hair hangs in clumps.
6. **Water colour changes with depth and light path.** Shallow reef reads pale teal over sand, deep water reads dark teal to cobalt (IMG-02), and the lip reads bright jade where light passes through it (IMG-17, IMG-15). Temperate water is olive-slate (IMG-07, IMG-09). See the measured palettes in section 7.
7. **Haze layers the coast.** Land behind the wave is always lower in contrast and bluer, or warmer at golden hour, than the foreground. That is aerial perspective plus salt haze (IMG-56, IMG-70, IMG-59, IMG-51). This is what makes the coast feel kilometres away, not like a backdrop.
8. **The foam field carries scale.** Big-wave frames read big because of foam texture and mist after the break (IMG-54, IMG-26, IMG-15), not only because of wave height.
9. **Spray and water on the lens appear in real follow-cam footage.** A few droplets or a brief splash on the lens signal "real camera". Use them sparingly, on wipeouts and in the barrel.
10. **The grade is natural, with lifted shadows.** No crushed blacks, and saturation is lower than people remember. Compare the measured palettes with the rejected "orange postcard" frames.

### Cheap vs expensive in WebGL2 (Three.js) **[Assessment]**

| Trait | Cost | How (sketch) |
|---|---|---|
| Level, lagged rear chase with an FOV and distance curve | **Cheap** | Damped springs on position and yaw, roll clamp, speed-based FOV and distance |
| Haze / aerial perspective over the coast | **Cheap** | Height and distance fog tinted by sun direction. ShoreBreak already has an HDR pipeline. |
| Bloom on backlit spray, plus exposure adaptation | **Cheap** (already in ShoreBreak) | Use its existing bloom, glare and auto-exposure. Tune thresholds against IMG-07 and IMG-75. |
| Colour grade per direction (palettes in section 7) | **Cheap** | LUT or lift/gamma/gain after tone-mapping. Must work with ShoreBreak's "phone look" tone shoulder. |
| Silhouette-first figure (strong pose, rim light) | **Cheap to moderate** | A rim/back-light term and correct exposure. Animation quality is the real cost. |
| Spray sheets and droplets (soft particles, a few thousand, crisp sprites, backlit forward scattering) | **Moderate** | GPU instanced billboards with a depth-fade and a phase function. Most of the "realism per ms". |
| Offshore lip spray (IMG-75) | **Moderate** | Emitter along the lip with wind-driven drift. Needs data from the wave team (lip position and normals). |
| Wet neoprene and skin (roughness and albedo masks, streaky speculars) | **Moderate** | Two-lobe specular or clearcoat in MeshPhysicalMaterial, plus animated wetness masks |
| Water on lens | **Moderate** | Screen-space droplet normal map with refraction, triggered by events |
| Wet hair clumps and secondary motion of boardshorts | **Expensive** | Cards plus a few bones or simple verlet. Avoid strand hair. |
| Realistic face and skin SSS | **Expensive, low value** | The camera is behind the surfer, so spend the budget elsewhere |
| Translucent lip / subsurface glow of the wave (IMG-17) | **Expensive, and owned by the ocean team** | Needs thickness-based transmission. Raise it with ShoreBreak early, because it is *the* signature of A and B. |
| Volumetric light shafts through broken cloud (C) | **Moderate to expensive** | Screen-space god rays are affordable. True volumetrics are not. |
| Motion blur | **Moderate** | Camera-only blur is enough. Per-object blur on spray would *hurt* realism (surf media freezes spray). |
